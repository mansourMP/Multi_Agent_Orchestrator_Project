**Scale-Readiness Verdict For “Thousands Of Users Today”**

No. The platform does not pass that threshold today.

The first failures are likely:
- read-heavy control-plane surfaces built on full `live_runs` scans
- notification and channel streaming loops at a few hundred concurrent clients
- workspace-route bootstrap traffic under normal navigation
- channel connector and outbox single-thread loops under bursty message volume

What does look better than the worst case:
- activity-ledger tables do have real indexes, so the stream problem is more poll frequency than raw schema absence: [`control_plane_repository.py#L646`](/Users/mansur/Multi_Agent_Orchestrator_Project/server_modules/control_plane_repository.py#L646)

**Confirmed Findings**
- `live_runs` is still the biggest shared hot table, and too many surfaces scan all of it.
- The active stream model is poll-heavy, not event-driven.
- Workspace bootstrap is expensive enough to matter under ordinary route traffic.
- Runtime target selection and registry seeding add real request-time overhead.
- Single-thread dispatch and single-thread delivery loops create head-of-line blocking.
- Some histories are bounded, but several hot paths still do full collection scans before applying user-facing limits.
